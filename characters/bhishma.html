Bhishma

भीष्म

The grandsire of the Kuru dynasty, bound by a terrible vow of lifelong celibacy.

Biography

Bhishma (originally Devavrata) was the son of King Shantanu and Ganga. To fulfill his father's desire to marry Satyavati, he took a terrible vow (bhishana pratijna) of lifelong celibacy and renunciation of the throne. He served the Kuru dynasty through three generations. In the Kurukshetra war, he led the Kaurava army for 10 days before falling on a bed of arrows, waiting until Uttarayana to die.

Major Events

1

The Terrible Vow

Renounced the throne and took a vow of celibacy so his father could marry Satyavati.

2

Abduction of Amba, Ambika, Ambalika

Won the three princesses of Kashi for his half-brother — but Amba loved another, leading to a tragic chain.

3

Kurukshetra War

Commander of the Kaurava forces for 10 days, fighting fiercely but not killing the Pandavas.

4

Fall on a Bed of Arrows

Pierced by Arjuna's arrows (with Shikhandi as shield), he lay on a bed of arrows, choosing to die at Uttarayana.

5

Vishnu Sahasranama

While on the bed of arrows, he recited the thousand names of Vishnu to Yudhishthira.

Important Dialogues

Bhishma— To Yudhishthira on the bed of arrows

“Duty (dharma) is subtle and difficult to discern. Even the wise are confused. I have served the Kuru house my entire life, bound by my vow.”

Mahabharata, Shanti Parva

Ethical Dilemmas

The vow of celibacy

His father Shantanu wanted to marry Satyavati, but her father demanded the throne for her children. Bhishma renounced everything.

Resolution

He took the vow, earning the boon of choosing his own time of death — but binding himself to serve whoever sat on the throne.

Fighting for the Kauravas

Bhishma knew the Pandavas were righteous, yet his vow bound him to the throne — which was held by Duryodhana.

Resolution

He fought for the Kauravas but vowed not to kill the Pandavas. He fell by his own choice, pierced by Arjuna's arrows.

Interpretations Across Traditions

Traditional

Bhishma is the ideal of duty and sacrifice — a man who gave up everything for his father's happiness and served the kingdom faithfully.

Critical reading

Bhishma's rigid adherence to his vow became a trap. His silence during Draupadi's disrobing shows the danger of duty without moral courage.
