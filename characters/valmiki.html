Valmiki

वाल्मीकि

The first poet (Adikavi), author of the Ramayana.

Biography

Valmiki was originally a highway robber named Ratnakara. He was transformed by the sage Narada's teaching of Rama's name. He meditated for years until ants built a mound (valmika) around him — hence his name. He is credited as the first poet (adikavi) and the Ramayana as the first poem (adikavya). He gave shelter to Sita during her exile and taught the Ramayana to Lava and Kusha.

Major Events

1

Transformation

From robber Ratnakara to sage Valmiki through Narada's guidance and meditation.

2

Composition of Ramayana

Inspired by Brahma, composed the first Sanskrit poem — the Ramayana.

3

Sheltering Sita

Gave refuge to the pregnant Sita when Rama abandoned her. Raised Lava and Kusha.

4

Recitation at Rama's Court

Lava and Kusha recited the Ramayana at Rama's court, leading to recognition.

Important Dialogues

Valmiki— Cursing the hunter who killed a crane

“You will not live long, O hunter — for you killed one of a loving pair. This became the first shloka (verse) of Sanskrit poetry.”

Ramayana, Balakanda

Ethical Dilemmas

Sheltering Sita

Valmiki knew Rama was righteous, yet Sita was suffering injustice.

Resolution

He sheltered her, raised her sons, and ensured the truth was known — demonstrating that even the righteous can err, and compassion must sometimes override authority.

Interpretations Across Traditions

Traditional

Valmiki is the Adikavi — the first poet, divinely inspired by Brahma to compose the Ramayana.

Academic

The Ramayana likely evolved over centuries; "Valmiki" may represent a tradition of bards rather than a single historical author.
